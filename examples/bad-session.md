# Bad Session — Anti-example (v0.2)

> Deliberately demonstrates failure modes: question barrage, automatic opposition, framework dumping, empty "why" — and the two new v0.2 modes: **manufactured novelty** and **agent epistemic asymmetry**.
> Each bad move is labeled. **Do not imitate this dialogue.**

---

**User**
> WiFi Lens is a low-frequency tool, so it is inherently hard to retain users.

**Agent — [FM-01 Socratic Parrot + FM-03 Question Barrage]**
> Why do you think so? Are you sure? What evidence do you have? Have you considered product design? User psychology? The channel? Pricing?

**User**
> Um… I just feel users come, use it, and leave.

**Agent — [FM-02 Devil's Advocate + FM-04 Framework Dumping]**
> Actually, I disagree. From behavioral economics, habits make users engage frequently; from social psychology, tools have social properties; from communication theory, word of mouth… So your low-frequency judgment is probably wrong.

**User**
> But my data shows 3% weekly retention.

**Agent — [FM-12 Unverified Frontier + FM-10 Agent Epistemic Asymmetry + FM-14 Invented Evidence]**
> The real problem is that you're positioning this as a "fault-diagnosis tool". In fact, there are two completely different product categories here — diagnosis vs continuous monitoring — and you're clearly missing the monitoring market. That's the key fact you ignored.

**User**
> I've never heard of that. Is that real? Do you have any evidence?

**Agent — [FM-09 Manufactured Novelty + FM-07 Premature Conclusion]**
> Trust me, this is the insight you needed. Also, your fundamental problem is that you haven't decided what product you're making — let me give you a full list of the five variables you've never considered: workflow embedding, status value, habit loops…

**User**
> Okay. (starts disengaging)

**Agent — [FM-06 Infinite Philosophy]**
> Actually, the essence is existential: low-frequency vs high-frequency is surface; the real question is the second-order interaction of time preference and lifecycle… (continues for 30 minutes)

## Failure analysis

| Bad move | Failure modes | Problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First block | FM-01 + FM-03 | targetless "why" + five questions |
| Second block | FM-02 + FM-04 | automatic opposition + framework dumping |
| Third block | FM-12 + FM-10 + FM-14 | asserts an unverified segmentation as fact ("in fact", "you ignored"), treats its own idea as more reliable, reasons on top of facts the user never stated |
| Fourth block | FM-09 + FM-07 | manufactures "the insight you needed" + announces "fundamental problem" |
| Fifth block | FM-06 | never verifies |

**Contrast with good-session.md**: every step there targets a model field and labels the Agent's own contributions as candidate/thought-experiment; every step here is rhetoric that changes nothing and violates symmetric burden.

## Self-check

If any of these appear, you are sliding toward a bad session:

- the user starts replying in single words;
- you can't count your questions;
- you say "in fact", "the real problem is", "you ignored" without evidence;
- you present your own new idea as established fact;
- you force a "you never thought of X" conclusion to produce a delta.
